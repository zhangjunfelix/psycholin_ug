## 🧠 Course Introduction

**Psycholinguistics** is the scientific study of how humans acquire, produce, understand, and represent language in the mind. This course introduces foundational theories, research methods, and key findings in the field, offering an integrated view of linguistic knowledge and language-processing mechanisms. Topics include speech perception, word recognition, sentence parsing, discourse comprehension, and non-literal language.

---

## 🎯 Course Objectives

By the end of the course, students will:
- Understand core psycholinguistic concepts and theoretical models.
- Describe major processes involved in language comprehension and production.
- Identify common methods used in psycholinguistic research.
- Critically evaluate experimental evidence and research findings.
- Apply insights from psycholinguistics to real-life language use and cross-linguistic scenarios.

## ✍️ Course Assignments

### 1. Class Participation (10%)

Active, thoughtful participation in class discussions and activities is required. Respectful engagement and preparedness are essential.

### 2. Quizzes (20%, 5% * 4)

Short quizzes on readings and key concepts.
- Quiz 1: on **Chapter 1, Chapter 2**
- Quiz 2: on **Chapter 3**
- Quiz 3: on **Chapter 4**
- Quiz 4: on **Chapter 7, Chapter 8**

### 3. Question Sets (20%, 10% * 2)

Two question sets are used to reinforce learning in different ways.
- **Question Set 1 — After class (10%)**: Learn about an experimental tool and use it to design a simple psycholinguistic experiment.
- **Question Set 2 — In class (10%)**: Questions and answers designed to check comprehension of course content.

### 4. Final Test (50%)

The final test will be administered in **Week 18**.

Students will answer **4–5 questions** based on the major topics covered in the course.
